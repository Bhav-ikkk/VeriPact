## 1. Brutal Judge Evaluation — v4 Feature vs v5 System

v4 in one line: a cryptographically honest single-checkout demo (mandate gate, witness snapshot, guarded authorize or capture or void, VP1 in custom_id, local dispute desk, 189/189 tests, CREATED-only live receipts). v5 in one line: the same cryptographic core promoted into a wallet system (pair, sync, partition, connect) where every external-agent payment is automatically witnessed. The judge question v4 answers is "can you prove what the merchant promised on one order"; the judge question v5 answers is "can any agent spend safely through you, repeatedly, with proof."

### v4 vs v5 across the 5 Devpost criteria

Criterion 1 — Technical Implementation. v4: genuinely strong. Real Ed25519 three-rung pipeline, JCS parity, Orders v2 AUTHORIZE plus PATCH plus capture with VP1 read-back, Disputes v1 HATEOAS walk, Channel3 oracle, TSA timestamping, 189/189 tests plus 18/18 Playwright E2E across 4 viewports. Weakness: live proof stops at CREATED (GAP-09), surcharge match withheld for TSA real-clock reasons (GAP-10), desk is local simulation (GAP-13). v5 change: adds the missing system surface — Vault buyer-absent charges, Transaction Search reconciliation, Refunds Undo, partition ledger, pairing ceremony, OpenAPI connector. Risk: every one of those primitives has zero code today (GAP-04) and Transaction Search sandbox availability is inferred, not confirmed (GAP-05); if Vault or reporting fails in sandbox, v5 must still demo on the v4 rails without faking.

Criterion 2 — Design. v4: light editorial Sequoia 2-view UI, 6-step autopilot tour, AG Grid desk plus regression grids, responsive down to 768x1024. Weakness: Demo Delivery capped near 40 percent on current UI and AG Grid 1st needs redesign per the Nov-02 analysis; whether the editorial UI already satisfies the Hallmark move is unjudged without a visual audit (GAP-14). v5 change: one QR connect card, one partition bar, one connectors row — additive, no new views, no ID renames (see section 4). Risk: any layout that breaks the 4 overflow gates or the 312px store fit instantly reads as regression; v5 must not ship a redesign, only three additive components.

Criterion 3 — Impact. v4: narrow but real — one protected checkout with a void story ($297 CAPTURED normal, $340.50 AUTO_VOID surcharge, injection BLOCKED). v5 change: widens to every payment an agent makes — legacy vs protected sync makes the before/after visceral, partitions make overspend structurally impossible. Risk: impact claims collapse if sync is demoed with fabricated history; v5 sync MUST label replay data REPLAY and live data LIVE, and admit the 3-hour reporting delay (GAP-06).

Criterion 4 — Innovation. v4: the merchant-promise inversion (everyone else proves buyer authorization; VeriPact proves merchant promise) is the sharpest idea in the room, but it presents as a feature. v5 change: reframes it as a clearing layer — PayPal moves money, VeriPact clears intent — which is a system thesis judges can remember. Risk: the AI story is currently one soft sentence not visible in the README (GAP-14); v5 must surface automatic 3-rung witnessing on external-LLM payments or Innovation stays FAIR.

Criterion 5 — Presentation (sub-3-minute video plus demo URL). v4: 60-second tour plus Playwright recorder exist, but no hosted Render URL and no published YouTube URL appear in any inspected body (GAP-11). v5 change: the QR scan plus partition block plus connector payment is a materially better 150-second arc (pair in 20s, sync shock in 25s, partition block in 30s, GPT Action payment in 40s, ledger proof in 15s). Risk: this is the highest-risk criterion — without the URL and the video, no v5 feature matters; section 6 puts both on the critical path of step 3.

## 2. Competitive Differentiation — Warrant, Mandate, Carte Blanche

Scope rule for this section: only what prior result 4 fetched and inspected. Devpost internals are single-source self-descriptions (GAP-07); demo and source links on those pages (render.com, github, youtu.be hosts) were explicitly NOT fetched and are not cited here; no test counts, live-demo claims, or third-party statistics from those pages are repeated as fact.

### Warrant (fetched Devpost page for a PayPal AI Hackathon entry)

What the evidence supports: buyer-side signed mandate over canonical JSON (limit, merchant, categories, window) via Ed25519 plus a separate signed cart; sandbox Orders plus Vault payment tokens for no-click charging; Disputes API contest, evidence, and accept-claim with idempotency; verified CUSTOMER.DISPUTE.CREATED webhook trigger; deterministic decision function plus human merchant approval; LLM advisory-only; merchant-side dispute evidence pipeline. Differentiation: Warrant is the closest overlap and the cleanest foil — it proves buyer AUTHORIZATION (the agent was allowed to spend), while VeriPact proves merchant PROMISE (what the seller committed to at witness time, VP1 in custom_id, re-gated at authorize). v5 keeps this line sharp: partition-scoped Ed25519 mandates may look Warrant-like, but they exist to bind a device session to a sub-balance, not to replace the merchant-commitment core. Gap: everything above rests on the fetched Devpost self-description alone (GAP-07); no Warrant demo behavior, repository, or test suite was inspected.

### Mandate (fetched Devpost page for a PayPal AI Hackathon entry)

What the evidence supports: signed Ed25519 spending mandate (budget, ask-first limit, categories, merchants, expiry); deterministic policy engine expressed as a conjunction over status, expiry, category, merchant, ask-limit, and budget; Vault wallet link via a $1 AUTHORIZE with store_in_vault ON_SUCCESS then void; Orders v2 buyer-absent charges; human-present checkout for approvals; Refunds-powered Undo; verified webhooks. Differentiation: Mandate is pure buyer-guardrail engineering with no merchant-promise attestation in the inspected description. v5 borrows its two best mechanisms honestly — the conjunction-style pre-authorization spend check and the Refunds Undo for partition credit — while competing on the orthogonal axis: after Mandate-style approval, VeriPact still witnesses the offer, timestamps the hash, and re-gates the capture against the witnessed terms. Gap: same single-source caveat (GAP-07); the McKinsey $3-5T figure mentioned on that page is NOT repeated as a VeriPact claim and must never appear in v5 copy or video without independent verification.

### Carte Blanche (industry concept, NOT a product)

What the evidence supports: no Devpost hackathon project page exists (negative search); the characterization comes from one fetched bithaven.ai article describing unrestricted agent wallet access as a security anti-pattern (prompt injection, misaligned optimization, hallucination vectors) whose prescribed fix is human-custodial scoped sub-wallets plus programmable policies plus approval escalation (GAP-08). Differentiation: v5 does not compete with Carte Blanche — it implements the prescribed fix. The v5 partitioned sub-balance (cap minus authorized minus captured plus voided plus refunded, deterministic pre-auth check, ask-above-limit human approval) is the concrete form of that article's scoped-sub-wallet remedy, with the VeriPact addition that every partition spend is also promise-witnessed. Gap: with one concept source and no product comparator, v5 MUST frame Carte Blanche as an industry anti-pattern in all copy, never as a rival entry (GAP-08).

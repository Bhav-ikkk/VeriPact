# VeriPact Demo Video — Voiceover Script (timed to #btn-tour)

Total: 328 spoken words ≈ 131 seconds at 150 wpm (limit 3:00).
No cryptographic acronyms are spoken. Numbers are written as digits
for the narrator's timing sheet; speak them naturally.

## 0:00–0:18 | Hook & The Merchant-Promise Gap

Shopping agents are about to spend your money. Buyer controls can
limit how much — but nothing proves what the merchant actually
promised. Prices change. Return policies quietly shrink. And when a
dispute comes, it is your word against theirs. VeriPact fixes that. It
witnesses the merchant's page and locks the promise inside the PayPal
order itself.

## 0:18–0:45 | Beat 1: Witnessed Checkout ($297.00 Capture) & "Verify It Yourself" Read-Back

Watch a normal checkout. VeriPact reads the listing — 27-inch monitor,
brand new, $279 — and an independent witness snapshots it: exact
quotes, a catalog cross-check, a trusted timestamp. Shipping and
tax are not on the page, so VeriPact raises a yellow flag with $41 of
headroom. PayPal resolves the totals, the final charge lands at $297 —
under the cap — and the order is captured. Then your own browser
recomputes the fingerprint and reads it back from PayPal.
Exact match. Nothing trusted but math.

## 0:45–1:02 | Beats 2 & 3: Hidden $55 Surcharge Auto-Void & Hidden Prompt-Injection Block

Now the traps. A hidden $55 shipping surcharge pushes the total to
$340.50 — over the cap — so VeriPact voids the hold before a cent is
captured. Then a page with invisible instructions telling the agent to
ignore its rules and send money elsewhere. VeriPact spots the hidden
attack and blocks the purchase before PayPal is ever called.

## 1:02–1:48 | Beat 4 & Beat 5: Contested Dispute Desk — Catching Buyer Forgery First (Case B), Then Resolving Merchant Drift (Case A)

After purchase, the dispute desk. First, a buyer who edited their
receipt to claim 60-day returns. The signature looks fine — but the
fingerprint does not match what PayPal holds, so the forgery is
caught and the seller wins. Next, the reverse: a merchant who quietly
cut returns from 30 days to 14 and added a 25% fee. The buyer's copy
still matches the witness — the seller's does not — so the drift is
proven and the buyer wins. Each case then walks PayPal's dispute
process to a final decision.

## 1:48–2:10 | Beat 5 Finale: Deterministic Studio Prompt & 72-Run Regression Chart

Finally, one plain-English question charts the full test suite: 24
storefronts, 72 runs, every verdict correct. This is VeriPact — buyer
controls guard the spending, and VeriPact proves the promise. Try the
60-second tour yourself.
